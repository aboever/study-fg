Tips for Studying

  • Self-grade honestly on the flashcards. The spaced-repetition scheduling only works if Again/Hard/Good/Easy reflect real recall difficulty — rating something "Good" that you half-guessed just hides a weak spot until the exam.
  • Interleave domains rather than blocking one at a time. Studying Domain E for three straight hours feels productive but tests recognition, not recall under mixed conditions — closer to what the actual exam will ask of you. Once the mixed-domain generator is built, use it daily rather than only working single-domain decks.
  • Weight your time toward Structure, Tectonics & Seismology (Domain E) and toward Mineralogy, Petrology & Geochemistry (Domain B). You flagged E as your weakest and B as borderline; both are also 12% of the blueprint each, so time there has real payoff. Hydrogeology (Domain F) is 13% but already your strength — a lighter maintenance pass there frees up hours for E and B.
  • Use the flagged-card panel weekly, not passively. Flag a card the moment something feels off, but actually revisit the flagged list on a fixed weekly cadence rather than letting it accumulate untouched until the end.
  • Cross-check flashcard recall against the official sample items. ASBOG publishes sample FG questions in the Candidate Handbook — cued recall (flashcards) and four-option multiple-choice under time pressure are different skills, and the exam only tests the second one.
  • Taper, don't cram, in the final 48 hours. The last two days are built into the plan below as review-only, no new material — the exam-date cap in the flashcard scheduling already guarantees nothing gets buried until after test day.
