01 / Geologic Properties, Soil Origins & Groundwater

G-1

1.1

Soil Origin: Residual vs. Transported

Soil forms in place by weathering of underlying bedrock (residual soil) or is moved and deposited elsewhere by an agent — ice (till), water (alluvium), gravity (colluvium/talus), or wind (loess) Marshak Interlude B, pp.155–161; transported-category terminology (till, alluvium, glacial drift) individually confirmed in Marshak Ch.6, Ch.18.

Partial gap: "colluvium" as a specific term did not surface in any search of the text — the concept (gravity-transported slope debris) is present under regolith/talus but not that label. Know the concept regardless of which term a question uses.
1.2

Terminology Trap: Two Unrelated "Soil Classification" Systems

Marshak's "Soil Classification" (Table B.2, p.159) is the U.S. Comprehensive Soil Classification System — a pedogenic/agronomic scheme (aridisol, mollisol, oxisol, etc.) describing soil formation environment for agricultural purposes. This is entirely unrelated to the engineering USCS (ASTM D2487/D2488 — GW, CL, CH, etc.) tested under G-2.2/2.3. FG questions on "soil classification" mean engineering USCS unless the context is explicitly pedologic.

1.3

Groundwater Fundamentals

Marshak's strongest chapter for this domain — full Darcy's Law derivation and terminology Marshak Ch.16, pp.473–494, Box 16.1; precise engineering-grade aquitard/aquiclude distinction from USBR Field Manual Vol.1 Ch.9, "Definitions".

TermDefinition
Porosity% void space — independent of permeability (e.g., clay: high porosity, low permeability)
Permeability / hydraulic conductivity (K)Ease with which water moves through a material
AquiferBody of soil/rock sufficiently permeable to yield usable water to wells/springs
AquitardConfining bed that retards but does not prevent flow — "leaky" confining bed
AquicludeRelatively impermeable body; does not transmit significant groundwater
Water tableTop of the zone of saturation; pressure = atmospheric

Darcy's Law: Q = K·A·(h₁−h₂)/L, where K = hydraulic conductivity, A = cross-sectional area, (h₁−h₂)/L = hydraulic gradient Marshak Box 16.1, p.479.

2.2/2.3

USCS Group Symbols & the Plasticity Chart

Two-letter USCS group symbol: first letter = dominant fraction (G-gravel, S-sand, M-silt, C-clay, O-organic, PT-peat); second = gradation/plasticity modifier (W-well graded, P-poorly graded, M-silty, C-clayey, L-low plasticity, H-high plasticity) USBR Field Manual Vol.1 Ch.3; ASTM D2487.

Plasticity chart (Casagrande chart) — directly confirmed tested: official ASBOG Candidate Handbook, Appendix 3, Q27 (a CH/LL/PI question).

  • X-axis: Liquid Limit (LL); Y-axis: Plasticity Index (PI = LL − PL)
  • "A"-line separates clays (on/above) from silts (below)
  • CL (lean clay): LL < 50, above A-line; CH (fat clay): LL ≥ 50, above A-line
  • ML (silt): below A-line, lower LL; MH (elastic silt): below A-line, high LL
  • Organic (OL/OH): oven-dried LL < 75% of not-oven-dried LL

Field identification tests (ASTM D2488): dry strength (crush a dried ball), dilatancy (shake a wet pat, watch water appear/disappear), toughness (roll a 1/8" thread near the plastic limit). Dilatancy is inversely related to plasticity — high dry strength + no dilatancy + high toughness → CH; low/no dry strength + rapid dilatancy + low toughness → ML. USBR Field Manual Vol.1 Ch.3; ASTM D2488

2.4/2.5

Compaction, Consolidation & Shear Strength

Mohr-Coulomb shear strength: τ = c + σₙ·tan(φ), where c = cohesion, σₙ = normal stress, φ = angle of internal friction FHWA-NHI-11-032, "Soils and Foundations" reference manual.

  • Compaction — mechanical densification (reducing air voids), typically for engineered fill
  • Consolidation — time-dependent settlement from expulsion of pore water under load; most significant in saturated fine-grained (clay) soils
2.6

Soil Slope Stability

Strong qualitative grounding: downslope vs. resistance force, angle of repose (30–37° typical, up to 45° for large angular grains), weak failure surfaces (wet clay, unconsolidated wet sand, joints, bedding, foliation) Marshak Ch.13, pp.406–413. Remediation techniques — terracing, drainage/lowering water table, retaining walls, riprap, rock bolts, revegetation — are Marshak-sourced directly Marshak Fig.13.17.

Factor of safety: FS = resisting forces / driving forces; FS < 1 indicates failure FHWA-NHI-11-032.

3.1

3.2

Discontinuities & Rock Quality Designation (RQD)

Marshak's "Joints" section explicitly notes geotechnical engineers pay close attention to jointing, and gives strike/dip terminology Marshak Ch.9, pp.268–271, Box 9.1. RQD and formal discontinuity description are a confirmed gap, fully supplemented from USBR Field Manual Vol.1 Ch.5, "Terminology and Descriptions for Discontinuities".

Discontinuity = umbrella term for structural breaks with zero-to-low tensile strength: fracture (joint, bedding-plane separation, random fracture — no significant displacement) vs. shear/fault (displacement occurred; evidence includes gouge, breccia, slickensides).

RQD calculation: sum lengths of intact core pieces ≥ 4 inches (100 mm) long, divide by total run length, ×100%. Mechanical breaks (drilling/handling artifacts) don't count — fit pieces back together and count as one if ≥ 4 in combined. Outcrop estimate (no core): RQD = 115 − 3.3·Jᷣ, where Jᷣ = joints per cubic meter.

RQD (%)Rock quality
<25Very poor
25–50Poor
50–75Fair
75–90Good
90–100Excellent

Deere (1964) five-band scale, per USBR Ch.5 / Deere & Deere 1989.

3.2/3.5

Discontinuity Description & the Shear-Strength Bridge

Description parameters: orientation, spacing (SP1 >10 ft to SP6 <0.1 ft), continuity/persistence, openness/aperture, filling (soil fillings classified by USCS), roughness (stepped→polished) and waviness, healing USBR Field Manual Vol.1 Ch.5.

Coulomb's equation for a discontinuity surface: S = C + N·tan(φ + i), where i is the roughness/waviness angle. A rough or wavy joint surface has higher effective shear resistance than a smooth one at the same normal stress — the direct mechanical link between discontinuity description (G-3.2) and strength (G-3.3) USBR Field Manual Vol.1 Ch.5.

3.3/3.6

Strength, Failure Criteria & Deformability

Marshak covers stress/strain fundamentals conceptually but no quantitative strength testing — confirmed gap, supplemented from peer-reviewed originals, appropriately, since these are specific named criteria still periodically revised.

  • UCS (uniaxial compressive strength) — ASTM D7012, axial load-to-failure, unconfined
  • Point load test — ASTM D5731, field-expedient index; correlates to UCS (UCS ≈ 20–25 × Iₛ₅₀, rock-type dependent, not a fixed constant)
  • Hoek-Brown failure criterion — empirical, nonlinear rock-mass strength envelope, the rock-mass analog to Mohr-Coulomb for jointed rock Hoek & Brown 1980, 1988, 1997
  • GSI (Geological Strength Index) — the field-observation input to Hoek-Brown, especially for weak/heterogeneous rock; estimated visually from rock-mass structure (blockiness) and discontinuity surface condition via a reference chart, not calculated Hoek, Carranza-Torres & Corkum 2002
  • Schmidt hammer — rebound hardness estimating UCS indirectly; average the 5 highest of 10 readings, discard the 5 lowest USBR Field Manual Vol.1 Ch.5
3.4/3.7

Failure Modes & Kinematic Analysis

Planar-failure concept only partially present in Marshak (Fig.13.12); wedge and toppling not addressed. Supplemented from the canonical source Hoek & Bray, Rock Slope Engineering, 1981; Goodman 1989 for toppling.

ModeKinematic condition
PlanarDiscontinuity dips out of slope face (within ~20° of slope dip direction); φ < dip of plane < dip of slope
WedgeTwo discontinuity sets intersect; line of intersection plunges out of slope face, gentler than slope but steeper than φ
TopplingDiscontinuities dip steeply into the slope (opposite direction); Goodman's criterion: (90° − dip) + φ < slope angle

Markland's test = stereonet procedure plotting the slope face as a great circle and the friction angle as a circle to check whether discontinuity poles/intersections fall within the sliding envelope.

05 / Investigation Methods

G-5

5.1

Well / Exploration Drilling Methods

Confirmed gap in Marshak, supplemented from USBR Field Manual Vol.1 Ch.8, "Exploration Drilling Programs".

  • Hollow-stem auger — preferred in soil/embankment work; doesn't pressurize the borehole (no hydrofracturing risk); hollow stem acts as casing for sampling
  • Direct rotary — mud, water, or air-foam circulation; standard for deeper/harder formations
  • Cable tool — percussion drilling, used where mud circulation is undesirable
  • Down-hole hammer w/ reverse circulation — hard rock, fast penetration + good recovery
  • Direct push — static force advance, fast, minimal disturbance, shallow/soft soils only
  • Sonic — high-frequency vibration, continuous core, works through mixed soil/rock/cobbles
Exam-relevant specific fact: augering is explicitly the preferred method for drilling in the impervious core of an embankment dam, because other rotary methods risk hydraulically fracturing the embankment.

06 / In-Situ & Lab Testing

G-6

6.1

In-Situ Testing: SPT & CPT

Confirmed gap, supplemented from ASTM D1586 (SPT); ASTM D5778 (CPT); USBR 7015 (Reclamation's own SPT procedure).

Standard Penetration Test (SPT) — the single most-tested in-situ method at FG level:

  • Drive a standard split-spoon sampler 18 in using a 140-lb hammer falling 30 in
  • Record blow counts per 6-in increment
  • N-value = sum of blows for the second and third 6-in increments (first 6 in — the "seating drive" — is discarded)
  • N correlates empirically to relative density (granular soils) and approximate undrained shear strength (cohesive soils)
  • Reclamation uses SPT data for dam liquefaction-hazard evaluation — direct tie to G-4

Cone Penetration Test (CPT) — continuously pushed at controlled rate (1.5–2.5 cm/s); measures tip resistance and sleeve friction continuously with depth; no physical sample recovered (key CPT-vs-SPT distinction); piezocone (CPTu) variants add pore-pressure measurement.

6.1

Groundwater / Aquifer Tests

USBR Field Manual Vol.1 Ch.9 covers instrumentation thoroughly (piezometer/observation-well design, water-level measurement — electric sounder, airline/gauge, popper, continuous recorders) but not pumping-test/slug-test analysis theory.

Confirmed gap: pumping-test/slug-test analytical theory (Theis, Cooper-Jacob) beyond definitional recognition is not sourced from anything pulled this session. At FG level: a pumping test estimates transmissivity/storativity by pumping a well at constant rate and observing drawdown in nearby observation wells; a slug test instantaneously changes water level in a single well and observes recovery for a localized permeability estimate.
6.2

Laboratory Testing

Confirmed gap, supplemented from FHWA-NHI-11-032; ASTM D2850/D4767 (scope only, standards paywalled); USBR Field Manual Vol.1 Ch.5 (tilt-test friction-angle estimation).

  • Triaxial test — confining pressure + axial load on a cylindrical sample; drained or undrained, with/without pore-pressure measurement; determines c and φ
  • Unconfined compression (UCS) — no confining pressure; simplest strength test, cohesive soils and intact rock
  • Direct shear test — shears a sample along a fixed plane under a range of normal stresses; builds a Mohr-Coulomb envelope directly. USBR Ch.5's "tilt test" is a field-expedient version for discontinuity-surface friction angle.
  • Drained vs. undrained — drained allows pore pressure to dissipate (slow loading, effective-stress c'/φ', long-term stability); undrained prevents drainage (rapid loading, total-stress parameters, short-term/construction-stage stability in saturated clays)
