Domain F — Hydrogeology

Specific capacity

Specific capacity = Q ÷ drawdown

Q = pumping rate (GPM). Drawdown = static water level − pumping water level.

Darcy's Law (discharge)

Q = K × i × A

K = hydraulic conductivity, i = hydraulic gradient, A = cross-sectional area.

Seepage (average linear) velocity

v = (K × i) ÷ ne

ne = effective porosity. Always greater than specific discharge (K×i alone) since flow is confined to pore space.

Hydraulic gradient

i = Δh ÷ L

Slope of the water table/potentiometric surface: head difference over distance.

Volume of water removed (unconfined aquifer)

V = Sy × Δh × A

Sy = specific yield, Δh = water table drop, A = aquifer area.

Theis dimensionless time parameter

u = (r² × S) ÷ (4 × T × t)

r = distance to observation well, S = storativity, T = transmissivity, t = time since pumping began.

Domain G — Engineering Geology

Relative compaction

Dry density = wet density ÷ (1 + water content) Relative compaction (%) = (field dry density ÷ lab max dry density) × 100

Slope grade and angle

Grade (%) = (V ÷ H) × 100 Angle = arctan(V ÷ H)

V = vertical rise, H = horizontal run (H:V slope ratio).

Factor of safety (slope stability)

FS = Resisting force ÷ Driving force Driving force = W × sin(θ)

W = weight of the mass, θ = slope angle. FS < 1 = unstable.

Active earth pressure force on a retaining wall (corrected — a practice-exam key error)

One exam's answer key gave 320 kN/m for this problem type — that's wrong. The key resulted from plugging the given pressure value directly into γ (which must be a unit weight, kN/m³) in the formula below, when the problem actually supplied a pressure (kN/m²) at the base. Those are different physical quantities with different units, and the substitution breaks dimensional consistency — it produces a number, not a real force.

Pressure at depth H: σa = Ka × γ × H   (kPa — a single point on the distribution) Total force (integrate the triangle): F = ½ × σa × H   (kN/m — force per unit length of wall)

Active pressure increases linearly with depth (0 at the surface, σa at the base) — a triangular distribution. Total resultant force is the area of that triangle: ½ × base × height = ½ × σa × H. If a problem already gives you the pressure at the base directly, use that value in the second formula — don't reach for γ and Ka separately, and never substitute a pressure value where the formula calls for γ. The resultant force acts at H/3 above the base (centroid of the triangle) — relevant if a question asks about overturning moment.

Worked example: H = 4 m, pressure at base = 40 kPa. F = ½ × 40 × 4 = 80 kN/m — not 320.

confirmed via units/dimensional analysis, cross-checked in a separate clarification session

RQD (Rock Quality Designation)

RQD (%) = (Σ core pieces ≥4in ÷ total core run) × 100

Domain A/E — Field Mapping & Structure

True (stratigraphic) thickness from outcrop width

T = W × sin(dip angle)

W = outcrop width measured on flat ground perpendicular to strike.

Apparent dip

α = arctan[tan(true dip) × sin(θ)]

θ = angle between true strike and the outcrop/cut strike (true strike minus outcrop strike).

Map scale conversion

New distance per inch = new scale denominator ÷ 12 (inches per foot)

E.g., 1:10,000 → 10,000 in ÷ 12 = 833 ft per inch. The old scale's distance-per-inch is irrelevant to this calculation — only the new scale's denominator matters.

Domain H — Economic Geology

Metal fraction within an ore mineral (by weight)

Metal fraction = atomic weight of metal ÷ molecular weight of the mineral compound

E.g., chalcopyrite CuFeS₂: Cu fraction = 63.5 ÷ (63.5+55.8+64).

Deposit economic value

Value = tonnage × ore grade × metal fraction × price per lb

Convert tonnage to lb (×2,000 or ×2,205 depending on short/metric ton) before applying price.

Time to reserve depletion

Time = (fraction of reserve × total reserve) ÷ production rate

Domain B — Mineralogy/Petrology/Geochemistry

Specific gravity of a rock (weighted average)

SGrock = Σ(mineral% × mineral SG) ÷ 100

Weight percent of one mineral in a rock

Wt% = (mineral% × mineral SG) ÷ Σ(all mineral% × SG) × 100

Denominator is the same weighted-sum as the specific-gravity formula above — compute it once, reuse for both.

Radioactive decay (fraction remaining)

Fraction remaining = (1/2)^(t ÷ half-life)

Solve for t (age) given the fraction remaining and known half-life — e.g., 25% remaining = 2 half-lives elapsed.

QAPF renormalization

Adjusted% = individual mineral% ÷ sum of the relevant apex minerals' % × 100

Required whenever one of the four QAPF apex minerals (Q, A, P, F) is absent or when only some are given — renormalize to just the minerals present before plotting.
